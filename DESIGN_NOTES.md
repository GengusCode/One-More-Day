# ONE MORE DAY — design research notes

## Direction

The game should combine the story variety of a choice-driven life simulator with the satisfying automation and visible growth of an idle-management game. Automatic work removes repetitive tapping; the player's job is to decide where money, time, and reputation should go.

## Lessons applied to this prototype

- **Choices should change the story.** Random life events affect money and personal stats rather than existing only as flavour text.
- **Automate repetition, preserve decisions.** The selected career works automatically. The player still chooses equipment, hiring, investment, risk, and whether to accept a new career opportunity.
- **Make milestones visible and rewarding.** Career experience has named ranks, a progress bar, reputation gains, and cash bonuses on promotion.
- **Use a few high-impact upgrades.** Equipment is now a single, career-specific purchase with a clear permanent benefit instead of a button bought repeatedly.
- **Let systems reinforce each other.** Knowledge and reputation unlock career opportunities; cash buys productive assets; workers turn a job into a business.
- **Keep mobile sessions short.** One day requires only a story decision and a strategic management check before advancing.
- **Give every day a story beat.** Each day now presents a different event or situation, even though routine work is automated.
- **Remember the player's history.** Careers already held are recorded and never presented as newly discovered opportunities.

## Next recommended test

The seven-day prototype is too short to reach every ownership tier naturally. The next test should expand a life to 30 days and measure whether promotions feel too fast or too slow before adding offline earnings or a prestige system.
